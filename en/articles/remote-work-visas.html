Remote Work

Digital Nomad and Remote Work Visas in Europe

6 min readUpdated 2026

If you work remotely for a company outside Europe, you don't need a traditional work permit to live there — but you do need a specific remote-work or "digital nomad" visa, which is a fairly recent and still-evolving category.

Remote Work

Which Countries Offer One

Portugal, Spain, Croatia, Greece, and Estonia are among the European countries with a dedicated remote-work visa, each allowing you to live locally while working for a foreign employer or as a freelancer with foreign clients — typically for one to two years, often renewable.

Typical Income Requirements

Most of these visas require proof of stable monthly income above a set threshold (commonly two to four times the local minimum wage), verified through recent bank statements and an employment or freelance contract. Some also require private health insurance and proof of accommodation before approval.

Tip: Requirements and thresholds change often as these programs are still relatively new — always check the current figures on the country's official immigration site rather than relying on older articles.

How It Differs From a Standard Work Permit

  • You're not employed by a company in that country, so there's no local employer sponsoring your permit.
  • You generally can't take on local clients or a local job under most remote-work visa terms.
  • Tax residency rules can get complex — you may become a tax resident of your host country after six months, even while employed elsewhere.

Is It Right for You?

This route only works if your current job or freelance income is genuinely location-independent. If you're looking to be hired by a European company directly, the standard work-visa pathways covered elsewhere on this site are the relevant route instead.
