Language Certificates

Which Language Certificate Do You Actually Need?

6 min readUpdated 2026

Picking the wrong language exam wastes both money and months. Each certificate is recognized differently depending on the country and purpose — visa, university, or employer requirement.

Language Certificates

German: TestDaF vs. Goethe-Zertifikat

TestDaF is generally required for university admission and is scored on a scale rather than pass/fail, making it useful for academic pathways. The Goethe-Zertifikat, issued at fixed levels (A1–C2), is more commonly requested for visa applications and skilled-worker permits, including the Chancenkarte points system. If your goal is a work visa rather than university, Goethe is usually the safer choice.

French: DELF/DALF vs. TCF

DELF and DALF are lifetime-valid diplomas issued at fixed levels (A1 to C2) and widely accepted for both visas and employment. The TCF (Test de Connaissance du Français) is scored, valid for two years only, and is what's typically required for specific immigration procedures like the Quebec pathway or certain French visa categories — check which one your target process actually asks for before registering.

English: IELTS vs. TOEFL vs. Cambridge

IELTS is the most widely accepted for UK and Ireland visa and immigration purposes. TOEFL is more common for university admissions, especially in international programs. Cambridge exams (like C1 Advanced) don't expire and are well recognized by employers directly, though less standard for visa applications specifically.

Before you register: confirm the exact certificate and minimum level required by the specific visa category, employer, or university you're targeting — requirements are rarely interchangeable, and taking the wrong exam means paying and waiting again.

General Tips for Any Language Exam

  • Book your exam date only after a practice test confirms you're near your target level — most certificates cost significantly more to retake.
  • Check the certificate's validity period; some expire after two years for immigration purposes even if the exam itself doesn't.
  • Keep official, certified copies — some authorities won't accept scanned results alone.
