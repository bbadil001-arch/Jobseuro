Working in Germany: Opportunities, Requirements, and Where to Look

8 min readUpdated 2026

Germany is one of the European countries with the greatest need for workers, driven by an aging population and skills shortages across several sectors — making it a realistic destination for many job seekers, provided you understand the rules well.

Germany

Sectors in Highest Demand

  • Healthcare and nursing: one of the most understaffed sectors, with pathways that ease recognition of foreign qualifications.
  • Engineering and tech: especially mechanical and electrical engineering, and IT.
  • Skilled trades (Handwerk): electricians, plumbers, carpenters, and auto mechanics — a traditional sector facing a severe shortage of skilled labor.
  • Hospitality: steady demand, especially in major tourist cities.

Why German Matters

Contrary to what some assume, English alone isn't enough outside major international companies and tech roles. A B1–B2 language level opens far more doors, especially in healthcare and skilled trades where daily communication with patients or colleagues is essential.

The Legal Pathway In

Germany offers several routes, most notably the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte), which lets qualified candidates enter to search for work under a points system considering age, qualifications, and language, alongside the EU Blue Card for degree holders, and direct work permits tied to a pre-signed contract in shortage occupations. There's also a formal process for recognizing foreign qualifications (Anerkennung) — essential in regulated professions like nursing and engineering.

Tip: Start your qualification-recognition process as soon as you settle on Germany, since it can take several months and is often a prerequisite before signing a contract in regulated professions.

Where to Look for Jobs

The official portal of Germany's Federal Employment Agency is a reliable starting point for officially listed jobs, alongside the EU's EURES platform. There are also government initiatives specifically aimed at foreign talent, outlining requirements per sector and the appropriate entry pathway.

Practical Steps to Get Started

  1. Check whether your profession is on an officially recognized shortage list.
  2. Start learning German to at least B1 alongside the other steps.
  3. Check the recognition process for your qualification if your profession is regulated.
  4. Search official platforms rather than relying solely on unverified middlemen.
