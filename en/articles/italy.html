Working in Italy: Opportunities, Requirements, and Where to Look

7 min readUpdated 2026

Italy's job market for non-EU workers runs on a distinct system compared to its neighbors — understanding the annual quota process is essential before you plan anything else.

Italy

The "Decreto Flussi" Quota System

Unlike Germany or France, Italy sets an annual government decree fixing how many non-EU work permits it will issue that year, split by category (seasonal, non-seasonal, self-employed). Applications open during specific windows and permits are granted on a first-come basis until the quota is exhausted — timing your application matters as much as your qualifications.

Sectors in Highest Demand

  • Tourism and hospitality: strong seasonal demand, especially in coastal and mountain regions.
  • Agriculture: a major share of the seasonal quota each year.
  • Healthcare and elderly care: steady demand given Italy's aging population.
Tip: Follow the official quota announcement dates closely — missing the application window often means waiting a full year for the next round.

Why the North Offers More Than the South

Milan, Turin, and the broader northern industrial belt have significantly more formal job openings and higher salaries than southern Italy, where the informal economy is larger and fewer positions go through official channels.

Practical Steps to Get Started

  1. Check the current year's Decreto Flussi quota and category deadlines.
  2. Target the north if you're prioritizing formal employment and salary.
  3. Learn at least basic Italian — it matters even in tourism roles dealing with international guests.
