Diploma Recognition

How to Get Your Foreign Diploma Recognized in Europe

6 min readUpdated 2026

A university degree earned outside the EU rarely transfers automatically. Whether you need formal recognition — and how long it takes — depends heavily on whether your profession is regulated.

Diploma Recognition

The ENIC-NARIC Network

Nearly every European country has a national ENIC-NARIC center responsible for evaluating foreign qualifications. These centers can issue a "statement of comparability" showing what your degree corresponds to locally — useful even for unregulated professions where an employer simply wants clarity on your education level.

Regulated vs. Unregulated Professions

In regulated professions — medicine, nursing, engineering, law, teaching, architecture — formal recognition is legally required before you can practice, and involves a specific national authority (not just ENIC-NARIC), often with additional exams or supervised practice periods. In unregulated professions — most business, marketing, IT, and creative roles — recognition is more about employer preference than legal requirement, and a strong portfolio or work history often matters more than a formal equivalence certificate.

Tip: Search for your specific profession plus "recognition" and your target country before assuming you need the full formal process — many roles don't require it at all.

Realistic Timelines

  • Unregulated professions: a comparability statement can often be issued within a few weeks to a couple of months.
  • Regulated professions: full recognition, including any required exams, commonly takes six months to two years depending on the country and profession.

What You'll Typically Need

  1. Certified copies of your diplomas and transcripts, often with sworn translation into the local language.
  2. Proof of the institution's accreditation in your home country.
  3. For regulated professions: proof of supervised practice hours or additional certification exams, depending on the field.
