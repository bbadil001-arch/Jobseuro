Nursing and Healthcare Jobs in Europe: A Sector Guide

7 min readUpdated 2026

Nursing and healthcare roles are among the most consistently in-demand professions across Europe, but the path to practicing legally involves more than just applying for jobs.

Healthcare

Countries Actively Recruiting Foreign Nurses

Germany, the UK, Norway, and Ireland run active recruitment programs specifically targeting foreign-trained nurses, sometimes including relocation support and language-course funding as part of the hiring package. Other countries hire steadily but with less structured international outreach.

The Licensing Exams Involved

Since nursing is a regulated profession everywhere in Europe, you'll need your qualification formally recognized (see our diploma recognition guide) plus, in most countries, an "adaptation period" or licensing exam that tests both clinical knowledge and language proficiency at a professional level — usually B2 or higher, since patient safety depends on precise communication.

Tip: Start your language study specifically around medical vocabulary early — general conversational fluency isn't the same as being able to pass a clinical licensing exam.

Realistic Salary Ranges by Region

  • Western Europe (Germany, Netherlands, Ireland): generally the highest nursing salaries, with a correspondingly higher cost of living.
  • Southern Europe (Spain, Italy, Portugal): lower salaries but often lower licensing barriers to entry.
  • Nordic countries: high salaries and strong working conditions, but a higher local-language bar in most roles outside major hospitals.

Practical Steps

  1. Contact the relevant national nursing/medical council early to understand your specific recognition requirements.
  2. Budget both time and money for licensing exams — some countries require multiple stages.
  3. Look for hospital groups with dedicated international recruitment teams, which often streamline the whole process compared to applying independently.
