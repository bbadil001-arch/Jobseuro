The Netherlands, Poland, and Spain: Alternatives Worth Considering

8 min readUpdated 2026

Focusing only on Germany and France means competing against the largest pool of applicants. Here are three other European countries worth a look, each with its own advantage.

Promising Alternatives

The Netherlands

Known for being open to foreign talent, especially in tech, logistics, and modern agriculture (greenhouse farming). Most international companies operate in English as their main working language, making it appealing for those who don't yet speak Dutch. There's also a simplified permit scheme for Highly Skilled Migrants at accredited companies, faster than the traditional routes.

Good fit if: you have a background in tech or logistics, and speak good English even without Dutch yet.

Poland

One of the fastest-growing European economies, facing a clear labor shortage in manufacturing, logistics, and construction, alongside a growing regional hub for business services and IT. Work-permit procedures are comparatively simpler than in Western Europe, and living costs are lower — making it a solid launchpad to build European experience before considering a move elsewhere in the EU.

Good fit if: you're looking for a faster, lower-cost entry point, and are open to starting in manufacturing or logistics.

Spain

The Spanish job market is especially active in tourism, hospitality, and agriculture, with growing demand in tech and renewable energy in major cities like Madrid and Barcelona. Spanish still matters in most roles outside international companies, but it's a relatively easy language to pick up for speakers of another Romance language. There are also pathways to regularize status for those who have resided a certain period and can prove a social tie or job offer — a mechanism that differs from the rest of Europe.

Good fit if: you have a background in tourism, hospitality, or agriculture, or want to learn a language you can pick up gradually.
