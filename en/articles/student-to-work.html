From Student to Employee: Converting a Study Visa Into a Work Permit

6 min readUpdated 2026

If you've studied in Europe, you're in one of the strongest positions to stay and work — most countries offer a dedicated bridge between a student visa and a work permit, but the window to use it is often shorter than people expect.

Students

Post-Study Job-Search Periods by Country

Germany allows up to 18 months after graduation to search for a job related to your degree. France offers a similar post-study residence permit of around 12 months. The Netherlands offers a one-year "orientation year" specifically for this purpose. These periods let you stay legally while job-hunting, without needing a job offer in hand the moment you graduate.

What Counts as a "Qualifying" Job

Most countries require the job you find to be relevant to your field of study, and often above a minimum salary or skill-level threshold — a job unrelated to your degree, or clearly below your qualification level, may not qualify you for the conversion even within the search period.

Tip: Start job-hunting seriously in your final semester rather than waiting until graduation — you'll have income and a converted permit lined up faster, rather than burning through your post-study search window.

The Practical Timeline for Switching Status

  1. Confirm your student visa's post-study provisions before graduating — not all study visas automatically include this right.
  2. Apply for the post-study residence permit immediately upon graduation, before your student status expires.
  3. Once you have a qualifying job offer, apply to convert your permit — processing time varies, so apply as early as your local rules allow.

Why This Path Is Often Easier Than Applying From Abroad

You already have language exposure, local networking opportunities, and — often — a local bank account and address, all of which make you a lower-friction hire for local employers compared to a candidate applying from outside the country.
